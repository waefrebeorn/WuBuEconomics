## Why the penny should come back (the economic case)

### 1. The rounding tax is a poor tax (filed ECO-1.1)

When the penny disappears, cash transactions round to the nearest
nickel. The Richmond Fed (2025) estimates a U.S. phase-out could
cost consumers **~$55.58 million per year**. Who pays it?

- Cash users are disproportionately low-income (unbanked,
  underbanked, unhoused).
- Merchants set prices; rounding systematically favors the party
  setting the price.
- The penny's production cost (~2–3¢) is replaced by an *invisible,
  permanent, regressive* tax.

**Bringing the penny back is the cheapest anti-poverty policy
nobody has proposed: it removes the rounding tax entirely.**

### 2. The penny anchors the cent

Every dollar is a hundred cents. Kill the cent's physical form and
the dollar's smallest unit becomes abstract. The space's doctrine:
**a currency needs a unit you can hold.** The penny is the holdable
cent. Without it, the smallest unit of the world's reserve currency
is a rounding error. With it, the cent has a body.

### 3. The penny has a fixed supply (filed RND-5, RND-7)

~114 billion pennies exist; the last was minted Nov 12, 2025; the
Common Cents Act (H.R. 3074, passed House July 2026) would make the
halt permanent. A currency unit with a *hard, physical, verifiable
cap* is the rarest monetary asset in history. Bringing the penny
back would mean minting new pennies *against the existing stock* —
adding liquidity without destroying the anchor class (the pre-1982
copper, which cannot be reminted by physics).

### 4. Intrinsic value is a psychological necessity

Weimar taught us (ECO-1.3): when money loses its felt value, faith
in everything priced in it collapses. The penny is the one coin
whose *felt* value (luck, memory, collection) exceeds its face
value. Restoring the penny restores the *feeling* of money — and
feelings are 90% of economics.

## The campaign (how we do it)

### Principle 1 — We do not gouge the penny

Explicitly filed: the space is **not** a penny-hoarding pump.
No speculation, no market cornering, no "penny shortage" stunts.
The campaign's goal is *stabilization*, not profit. A penny that
is worth $1 as a collectible has failed as currency. A penny that
is worth 1¢ — and *always will be* — has succeeded.

### Principle 2 — We file with the government, not against it

- We obey every law (RND-5: §486, melting ban; RND-6: the audit;
  RND-7: the classes).
- We petition through proper channels (see the petition below).
- We publish our reasoning, our sources, our math — all public,
  all cited. A campaign that hides is a conspiracy; a campaign
  that files is a proposal.

### Principle 3 — Representation in whimsical collection

The campaign's cultural engine (filed ECO-1.7): people collect what
represents them. The space gives the penny a *collection* — the
Registry's citizens, the PennyChain's proofs, Penny Day's novelty
pieces. Every citizen who sends a compliance penny is a card in the
space's deck. The penny stops being "the coin they want to kill"
and becomes "the coin we all hold." **Cultural pressure through
affection, not anger.**

### Principle 4 — Penny Day is the megaphone

Any issue with the penny plan → **Penny Day** (RND-16): novelty
coinage, celebration, the dispute becomes a holiday. Penny Day is
the campaign's loudest voice: it shows the country (and the world)
that the penny is loved, held, weighed, chained, and *wanted*.
You cannot kill a coin that has a holiday.
